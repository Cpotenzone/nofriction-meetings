From BASIC to billions

No Friction was established by Casey Potenzone, whose technology journey began in 1977 near New York City. His early experience with computing started at age 9, programming a pixelated spacecraft animation in BASIC on an Epson 8088. Following a brief period at NYU, where his focus gravitated more toward computer labs than lecture halls, Casey entered the DotCom era at a pivotal moment, positioned at the intersection of two technological revolutions.

  1. 01

    Early days: the coding genesis

    1986–1994

    My relationship with technology began when other kids were collecting baseball cards. There I was, age 9, hunched over an Epson 8088, copying BASIC code from a book because YouTube tutorials didn't exist. Each successful program felt like winning the lottery, especially when my screensaver actually spun.

    The future belongs to those who learn to code, or those stubborn enough to keep trying after their program crashes for the 27th time.
    • First programming experience on an Epson 8088
    • Self-taught BASIC from technical manuals
    • Pioneered using websites for college applications
  2. 02

    DotCom revolution and MTV

    1995–2000

    After realizing college lectures couldn't keep pace with the internet explosion, I left NYU to dive into the DotCom madness. By 20, I'd somehow convinced Chase Bank I knew what I was doing, then stumbled my way into MTV.

    At MTV, we didn't just adopt technology. We broke it first, fixed it frantically before anyone noticed, then claimed we meant to do that all along.
    • Early career at Chase Bank and Havas
    • Pivotal role at MTV during its digital transformation
    • Pioneered early streaming media solutions
  3. 03

    Innovation and patents

    2000–2007

    I wrote my first patent at 21, an application for managing our streaming solutions at MTV. We effectively wrote what became foundational for modern playlist management. By 30, I had another patent for code security.

    • First patent at 21 for streaming media management
    • Security-focused patent at 30
    • Fintech, e-commerce and product development experience
  4. 04

    Trial by fire: executive leadership

    2008–2015

    Found myself managing a quarter-billion dollars in yearly transactions at a company the FTC suddenly took interest in. Nothing makes you update your LinkedIn profile faster than seeing government agents in the lobby.

    Leadership isn't proven during easy times. It's when you're explaining complex regulatory frameworks to angry board members while trying not to spill coffee on your only good suit.
    • Managed $250M+ in yearly transactions
    • Navigated complex regulatory challenges
    • Developed crisis management expertise
  5. 05

    Global leadership and family

    2012–2020

    Somehow convinced my amazing wife to marry me in 2012, and we welcomed our daughter shortly after. Built teams across Europe, the GCC and the US, mostly by admitting I didn't have all the answers and hiring people smarter than me.

    • Built international teams across multiple continents
    • Balanced global business leadership with family life
    • Developed personal resilience through endurance sports
  6. 06

    No Friction: IP monetization, then an app

    2021–present

    After decades of making mistakes and occasionally learning from them, I started No Friction to help companies make money from their IP without the usual headaches. Then I built the tool I wanted for my own days: noFriction, the recorder that keeps everything on your device.

    The second technological revolution of my career is underway, though this time I'm slightly more prepared and significantly more caffeinated.
    • Founded No Friction and the 'High-Touch, Digital-First' methodology
    • AI implementation in IP management
    • Built the noFriction app for iPhone, Apple Watch and Mac

Community. I've supported organizations from UN non-profits to local charities over the years, partly because it's the right thing to do, and partly because karma points might come in handy someday.

"I'm basically a film editor for technology: cutting, splicing, and occasionally adding special effects to make the storyline better. Sometimes there's popcorn involved."
